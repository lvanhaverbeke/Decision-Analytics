Brussels room investment: executive report

14 October 2026 | Pre-tax, unlevered feasibility screening

At the stated EUR 15 million construction budget, the model returns NPV EUR 179,925,426, project IRR 33.5%, simple payback 4.18 years and discounted payback 4.49 years from initial investment. The three requested financial tests pass at a 4.30% discount rate.

Recommendation: proceed to a feasibility and contractor cost assessment before committing to construction. The budget is EUR 7,500 per room; it is an input, not an independently supported construction estimate.

The project builds for two years with EUR 7.5 million paid at t=0 and t=1. It opens at t=2, with first full-year rental cash received at t=3. Opening rent is EUR 450 per room per month and grows 5% each operating year. At 95% occupancy, the first operating year generates EUR 10.26 million rent and EUR 6.669 million cash after 30% operating costs and a 5% maintenance reserve.

The model uses 30 operating years, zero terminal proceeds, and no borrowing or income tax. Land, VAT, professional fees and other omitted upfront items must be entered in Inputs B13. A risk premium can be added in Inputs B16. The sovereign yield is an official reference, not an authority-prescribed real-estate investment discount rate.

A fixed 5% rent escalation is the user scenario. Brussels residential indexation depends on the health index and lease requirements; it is not automatically a fixed 5% increase. Opening rent is capped at EUR 450; subsequent affordability limits are assumed to rise with inflation.

Next steps: validate site and planning capacity, floor area, permits, all-in construction costs, land and tax treatment, occupancy, owner-paid utilities and operating contracts; then rerun the financial and payback tests.
